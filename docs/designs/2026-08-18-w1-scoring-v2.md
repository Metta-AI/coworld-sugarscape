# Target-scaled distribution scoring

Status: implemented and calibrated; approved for rollout in a new season.

## Problem

Sugarscape currently reports `1 - W1 / support_width`. The support width is a
binning boundary, not a measure of the target's characteristic variation. For
the wealth targets it is 1000 sugar, so destroying the target's shape at a raw
transport cost of about 40 sugar still scores about 0.96. That number is
mathematically consistent with the implementation but misleading as a match
score.

The replacement must preserve bin geometry, provide useful selection pressure
when an outcome is far from its target, work for single-bin targets, and have a
scale that comes from the target rather than an unrelated declared support.

## Decision

Let `D` be the raw binned Wasserstein-1 distance:

```text
D = sum_i |CDF_measured(i) - CDF_target(i)| * bin_width(i)
```

For each bin `i`, let `delta_i` be a point mass in that bin. Choose the smallest
index `i*` minimizing `W1(delta_i, target)`, and define the target scale:

```text
S = max(W1(delta_i*, target), bin_width(i*))
```

The first term is the target's binned mean absolute deviation from its median.
The bin-width floor keeps the scale positive for a one-bin target and says that
one bin of displacement is one resolvable unit. Smallest-index tie breaking is
part of the contract because tied median bins can have different widths.

The ranked score is:

```text
score = S / (S + D)
```

It is 1 only for an exact histogram match, 0.5 at one characteristic target
scale of transport, strictly decreases as raw W1 increases, and approaches zero
without a clipped zero plateau. Empty measurements and seats that die before the
end retain their existing hard-zero rules.

The method identifier is `w1-hyperbolic/1`. Raw `D`, `S`, and base-2
Jensen-Shannon divergence are diagnostics; Jensen-Shannon is not part of the
ranked score.

## Alternatives considered

### Keep support-normalized W1

Rejected. The 1000-sugar wealth support makes approximately 40 sugar of
transport look like a 0.96 match. Changing support bounds would also change
scores without changing either histogram.

### Linear target-MAD skill score

`max(0, 1 - D / MAD(target))` gives the desired harsh interpretation for a
median collapse, but is undefined for a one-bin target and creates a broad
zero-gradient plateau. In the 24-scenario corpus it was undefined for every
population scenario and clipped price, survivorship, and scarce-world outcomes
to zero.

### Jensen-Shannon similarity

Rejected as the primary score. Jensen-Shannon compares corresponding bin mass
but does not know that two bins are adjacent. In the population compact, sparse,
and wide scenarios it scored both the null outcome and an evolved one-bin-closer
outcome at zero similarity. This erases exactly the geometry-aware learning
signal the game needs.

### Product of Wasserstein and Jensen-Shannon similarities

Rejected. A product inherits Jensen-Shannon's zero-signal cases and makes the
tradeoff between shape and displacement difficult to explain. The probe corpus
showed no compensating benefit.

### Normalize against each scenario's null policy

Retained only as a probe diagnostic. It changes the meaning from absolute target
match to improvement over one policy, is undefined when the null policy already
matches, and would require scenario-specific calibration.

## Calibration evidence

The initial comparison rescored null, greedy, and stored evolved rulesets for
all 24 solo-ladder scenarios on seeds 11 and 42 (144 episodes). Representative
null-to-evolved changes under the selected score were:

| Scenario | Null | Evolved | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| population compact/sparse/wide | 0.3333 | 0.5000 | +0.1667 |
| price four markets | 0.1952 | 0.2713 | +0.0762 |
| price split centers | 0.1952 | 0.3086 | +0.1134 |
| survivorship long-lived | 0.3317 | 0.4542 | +0.1225 |
| tribe convergence three-way | 0.4624 | 0.8562 | +0.3937 |
| wealth egalitarian four-corners | 0.6233 | 0.9111 | +0.2877 |
| wealth skew four-corners | 0.6217 | 0.9266 | +0.3048 |
| wealth skew twin-peaks | 0.8622 | 0.9661 | +0.1039 |

The replay that triggered this investigation has `D = 39.827733...`,
`S = 33.711467...`, and therefore scores `0.458415...`, rather than 0.960.

A second run evaluated fixed null and stored-best policies on five seeds. It
found clean signal in population, survivorship, and wealth, but high native seed
variance in price and tribe scenarios. Paired-seed confidence analysis separated
real policy effects from shared world variance: for example, price split-centers
had a +0.1235 mean paired improvement with a +0.0358 one-sided 95% lower bound,
while price four-markets had a -0.0224 lower bound and tribe convergence a
-0.1467 lower bound. Repeating the same analysis under legacy support-normalized
W1 produced the same noisy cases and the same tribe-diversity reversal. The new
transform did not create the instability; the old two-seed probe hid it.

## Optional reachability calibration

The old universal score-at-least-0.50 interpretation cannot simply be carried
forward:
0.50 now has the meaningful but stricter interpretation `D = S`. A scenario is
acceptable when either:

1. its evolved ceiling is at least 0.50 (the anchor arm), or
2. its evolved ceiling minus null floor is at least `G` (the skill arm).

The implemented gate uses disjoint evolution and evaluation seeds. On at least
five held-out seeds, let `C` be the scores of the stronger absolute anchor
candidate (null or evolved, selected by held-out lower confidence bound), and
let each paired `delta_s = best_s - null_s`. Including null in the anchor arm
matters when evolution overfits its training seeds: a scenario that the fixed
null policy reaches must not fail solely because the evolved candidate
generalizes poorly. A scenario passes when either:

1. `mean(C) - t * SE(C) >= 0.50` (anchor arm), or
2. `mean(delta) >= 0.03` and `mean(delta) - t * SE(delta) > 0` (skill arm),

where `t = 2.131846786`, the one-sided 95% Student-t critical value for five
observations. Using that value with more observations is conservative. Pairing
cancels common seed variation; the 0.03 materiality floor prevents a
deterministic but negligible change from passing. Failing both arms is a useful
signal that a scenario is both far from its target and not demonstrably
responsive to policy. It is not a release gate for this metric migration: the
existing pool predates the new scale, and targets are allowed to be
aspirational. Per-scenario production distributions should be monitored after
the season reset, and only empirically inert, trivial, or excessively noisy
scenarios should be retuned.

The two predeclared anchor candidates make this an operational 95% gate rather
than a family-wise 95% test: accepting either lower bound mildly increases the
false-positive rate because both candidates use the same held-out seeds. Reports
therefore record whether null or the evolved policy carried the anchor arm. A
null-carried anchor is a regression guard; an evolved-carried anchor is reachable
but did not demonstrate the paired skill effect under this probe budget.

## Runtime safety

SugarLang cannot loop or execute player code. Validation bounds a submission to
256 expression nodes, depth 16, and 32 KiB; every primitive is constant-time,
and evaluation occurs over a finite candidate set for a finite agent population
and 1,000 ticks. A deliberately expensive valid 256-node expression completed
the full four-basins scenario locally in 64.428 seconds: 56.304 seconds in the
simulation, including 38.147 seconds of ruleset evaluation, plus 8.074 seconds
of replay serialization. The exact initial 24-policy probe population contained
1-to-47-node rulesets and showed no runtime outliers at 100 ticks.

Do not add an in-simulation wall-clock cutoff. That would make the same seed and
ruleset produce different results on different hardware, violating deterministic
replay and scoring. The hosted job deadline remains an infrastructure containment
boundary, not a game rule. Even assuming a conservative 4x hosted CPU slowdown,
the measured adversarial episode projects to about 258 seconds, leaving roughly
3.5x headroom inside a conservative 900-second compute allowance after startup,
submission, shutdown grace, and artifact handling. If that margin narrows, use a
deterministic expression-evaluation budget or lower the static node limit rather
than a clock. A hosted-class adversarial measurement remains a worthwhile rollout
check.

## Artifact and compatibility contract

- Results add top-level `score_method`. Per-seat details replace the ambiguous
  normalized `w1` with `raw_w1` and `w1_scale`; `js_divergence` remains.
- `coworld_manifest.json` requires `score_method`, preventing a new build from
  silently omitting the semantic version.
- New replay headers carry `score_method`; the replay envelope remains version
  2 so existing recordings remain loadable.
- A replay with no method is interpreted by the viewer as legacy
  `w1-support/1`. An unknown method is not guessed: viewer re-scoring returns no
  value while recorded engine scores remain available.
- Target JSON files do not store a redundant scale. The engine and viewer derive
  it deterministically, while tests pin every shipped target's value so target
  regeneration makes scoring changes visible in review.

Changing the ranked method invalidates direct comparison with legacy leaderboard
scores. The rollout decision is a new season rather than a historical backfill,
making the semantic break explicit.

## Validation evidence

1. Done: compare fixed null and evolved policies across five seeds and repeat
   the paired analysis under the legacy method to rule out transform-induced
   noise.
2. Done for the metric decision: run focused fresh evolutionary probes under
   the new objective for
   `survivorship.young-frontier`, `wealth-egalitarian.four-basins`, and
   `capacity.compact-regrow-1`. These cover the far tail, near-target band, and
   deterministic one-bin geometry. Capacity and young-frontier demonstrated
   useful gradients; four-basins runtime investigation showed that its silent
   batch was aggregate work rather than a pathological policy.
3. Done: pin engine/viewer parity across every shipped target, delta masses at every
   bin, empty measurements, and real episode histograms. Test both legacy and
   new replay method dispatch plus unknown-method fail-closed behavior.
4. Done: run a duo episode and verify each seat reports the scale derived from its own
   target.
5. Waived as a release gate: do not repeat 24 full evolutionary searches merely
   to migrate the score. Existing null, greedy, and stored-policy rescoring is
   the calibration baseline; use production per-scenario distributions to drive
   later retuning.

The first exposed calibration change was `wealth-skewed.scarce-lowland`: its old
0.960 support-normalized score became an honest 0.458 with zero measured policy
gradient. The scenario is intentionally unchanged in this scoring-only rollout;
production evidence, rather than a threshold inherited from the legacy scale,
will determine whether it should later be retuned.

The one-generation objective smokes established that selection still climbs
the new transform. Capacity independently rediscovered the stored optimum
(0.500 versus null 0.333) on every held-out seed. Young-frontier reached 0.282
versus null 0.239, with a +0.039 paired lower-bound improvement, and passes the
skill gate; as expected from the reduced budget, it did not yet match the old
12-generation best at 0.294. The four-basins smoke was stopped after more than
eight minutes without completing its initial random population. Reproduction
showed no pathological generated policy: the probe had scheduled 72 full
replay-producing episodes without intermediate progress. That finding does not
qualify or disqualify the metric.

Historical backfill is intentionally not part of this rollout.
